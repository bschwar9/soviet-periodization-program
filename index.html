About 1RM estimation

If you enter more than one rep, your max is estimated with the Brzycki formula: 1RM = weight × 36 / (37 − reps). Estimates get less reliable past 5 reps, and neural efficiency varies a lot between lifters. Two people with the same 5-rep max can have 1RMs 10–15% apart. A tested single, double or triple gives the most accurate program.

Light days

Use the 6×2 at 80% days to refine technique. Take full 2-second pauses at the bottom with a slow, controlled descent. You control the weight; don't let it control you.

Day 2 is a pause bench, close-grip bench or Spoto press (each rep paused just above the chest) at 70%. Keep it easy. Rest 2–3 minutes between sets.

Where this comes from

A.S. Medvedev was a Soviet weightlifting champion turned sports scientist at the Central Institute of Physical Culture in Moscow. Building on Leo Matveyev's periodization framework, he found most training volume belongs in the 70–85% of 1RM range: heavy enough to drive adaptation, light enough to accumulate volume without excess fatigue. This program follows that approach with moderate weights, undulating rep schemes and a taper into a peak.

A.S. Prilepin analyzed the training logs of over 1,000 Soviet weightlifters to find optimal volume ranges at each intensity. The resulting Prilepin's Chart underlies this program's structure. Yuri Verkhoshansky developed block periodization and the shock method (depth jumps), shaping how athletes sequence training phases.
